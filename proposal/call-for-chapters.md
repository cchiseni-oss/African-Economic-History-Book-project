### About the volume

Research on Africa's economic history has grown fast, but few of the people writing it are based in Africa. Of the papers on Africa published in the top four economic history journals in the two decades to 2019, 13.5 per cent were written by scholars based in Africa (Fourie 2019).

*From Within* is an edited volume that brings together African economic historians, on the continent and in the diaspora, to write the continent's economic history from African vantage points. It will cover the main development themes of the field from pre-colonial times to the present. The editors intend to publish it open access, with an affordable print edition for African readers, so that it can be used as a core text in African universities as well as a reference for researchers and policymakers.

### What we mean by "African perspectives"

We do not expect contributors to share one view, and we welcome debate. We do ask every chapter to:

- start from questions that mattered to the African people and societies involved;
- combine quantitative or qualitative evidence with African archives, oral histories, African-language sources or material evidence where possible, and explain the limits of colonial-era records;
- treat Africans as economic actors, with attention to differences of gender, class, region and generation;
- trace how the past connects to the present through the periods in between, rather than jumping from a distant cause to a modern outcome;
- engage with African scholarship, including work in French, Portuguese and Arabic and work published by African presses.

Cliometric, qualitative and mixed-methods approaches are all welcome.

### Chapter themes

We invite proposals on the themes below. Proposals on other themes, such as urbanisation and the informal economy, business history, Islamic economic institutions, land tenure, demography and health, war, migration and remittances, or North Africa and the Nile valley, are also welcome.

**Knowledge, sources and method**
1. African traditions in economic history: Ibadan, Dar es Salaam, CODESRIA and the recent quantitative revival
2. Sources, silences and numbers: building evidence from African archives, oral histories and statistics

**African economies before colonial rule**
3. Land, labour and ecological knowledge in production systems
4. Markets, merchants, money and states: Africa's commercial networks
5. The slave trades from within: demographic, political and economic consequences

**Colonial economies and African responses**
6. Taxation, chiefs and fiscal extraction
7. Mines, migrants and labour regimes
8. Peasants, export agriculture and marketing boards
9. Missions, schools and clinics: human capital and inequality *(chapter by the editor)*

**Independence, crisis and reform**
10. Nationalisation, African socialism and the developmental state
11. Debt, structural adjustment and policy sovereignty, from the Lagos Plan to the Common Framework
12. Women's work, markets and property

**Long-run trajectories: Africa and the world**
13. Money and monetary sovereignty: currency boards, the CFA franc, savings groups and mobile money
14. Roads, rails and regions: infrastructure and spatial inequality
15. Climate, famine and adaptation
16. From customs unions to the AfCFTA: regional integration in the world economy

### Who can apply

- Lead authors should be African scholars, by nationality or heritage, based on the continent or in the diaspora. Co-authors from elsewhere are welcome where they bring specific expertise.
- We particularly encourage proposals from scholars based at African institutions, from women, from early-career researchers (including advanced PhD candidates), and from scholars working on Francophone, Lusophone, Arabic-speaking and North African contexts.
- Early-career contributors will be paired with a senior mentor.
- Contributors writing in English as a second or third language will receive language-editing support [subject to funding].

### What each chapter will include

- 6,500–7,500 words, including notes and references
- A 300-word "Sources and silences" box on the evidence used and what it leaves out
- Three to five discussion questions and five annotated further readings
- Two to four maps, figures or tables
- An abstract in English and French

Chapters must be original work not published elsewhere. Chapters may build on a contributor's existing research.

### How to apply

Send the following, as a single PDF or Word file, to **[email address]** with the subject line "From Within proposal: [your surname]":

1. A chapter title and a proposal of up to **500 words** setting out the question, the argument, the sources and the method, and which theme it addresses
2. A short bio of up to **150 words** for each author, with institutional affiliation and nationality or heritage
3. A list of up to five relevant publications or works in progress

Proposals may be written in English or French.
