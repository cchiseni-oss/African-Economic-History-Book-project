## 2. Publishers

### 2.1 Recommended shortlist

1. **University of Rochester Press: *Rochester Studies in African History and the Diaspora*** (Boydell & Brewer). Series editor Toyin Falola. The series lists interests that match this book closely, including economic activity during the colonial period, economic development modelling for Africa, and historiographical studies of particular regions. The press says the series brings Africa-based voices into the mainstream academy. Boydell & Brewer accepts open-access proposals for this imprint. [Series page](https://boydellandbrewer.com/rochester-studies-in-african-history-and-the-diaspora/) · [Series description](https://www.cambridge.org/core/series/rochester-studies-in-african-history-and-the-diaspora/F52BFC21B074F63F9B3F7DB4350F0F83) · [Africa-based voices](https://africanstudies.secure-platform.com/a/gallery/rounds/7/details/1538) · [Open access at B&B](https://boydellandbrewer.com/blog/other/boydell-brewer-open-access/)
2. **James Currey** (Boydell & Brewer). An imprint of Boydell & Brewer since 2008 and one of the best-known academic publishers on Africa, covering history, economics, development and environmental studies. Accepts open-access proposals. [Imprint page](https://boydellandbrewer.com/james-currey) · [Proposal guidance](https://boydellandbrewer.com/submitting-a-book-proposal/) · [40 years of James Currey](https://boydellandbrewer.com/blog/african-studies/celebrating-40-years-of-james-currey/)
3. **Cambridge University Press: *African Studies* series, or a stand-alone edited volume.** The series, founded in 1968, publishes monographs, surveys and textbooks in history, political science, anthropology and economics. CUP published the closest comparable title (Akyeampong et al. 2014) as a stand-alone volume. This is the most prestigious option on the list; print prices are high and open access requires a fee. The CUP Author Hub lists Maria Marsh as the editor for this list; check that this is still current. [Series page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/series/african-studies/AB4FF0D3094511B59CFEFCCC124450A6) · [Author Hub](https://www.cambridge.org/authorhub/editors/maria-marsh)
4. **Palgrave Macmillan** (Springer Nature): *African Histories and Modernities* (series editors Toyin Falola and Matthew M. Heaton), or *Palgrave Studies in Economic History*, which covers financial and labour history, development economics and globalisation. Your 2024 open-access monograph is with Springer Nature, so you already know its process, and Springer Nature runs a free service to help authors find open-access funding. [African Histories and Modernities](https://link.springer.com/series/14758) · [Palgrave Studies in Economic History](https://link.springer.com/series/14632) · [Open-access books at Springer Nature](https://www.springernature.com/gp/open-research/journals-books)
5. **An African or no-fee open-access press**, as main publisher or as co-publisher with one of the above: CODESRIA, African Minds, HSRC Press, Wits University Press or Open Book Publishers (details in 2.2).

Toyin Falola edits both the Rochester and the Palgrave series. Approach one of them first, or ask him which suits the book better.

### 2.3 Approaching publishers

- Start with a one-page enquiry to two or three commissioning or series editors: the summary, the table of contents and your biography. Ask whether they would like the full proposal.
- Expect to be asked for the full proposal, the editors' CVs, a list of confirmed contributors and a sample chapter, usually the introduction.
- Edited volumes are harder to place than monographs. Confirmed contributors, a senior co-editor, a clear common thread and evidence that courses would adopt the book all help.
- Ask each press about open-access fees and waivers, African pricing or co-publication, word and figure limits, and production time.
- If you send the proposal to more than one press at once, tell them, and check each press's policy on this.
- If no press takes the book, a themed special issue of a journal such as *Economic History of Developing Regions* can test the idea and build the contributor group. It is the journal of the Economic History Society of Southern Africa, co-published with UNISA Press, and you already publish and review there. [Journal page](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/rehd20)

### 3.4 Open access

- Commercial presses (Springer Nature and Palgrave, Cambridge, Routledge, Boydell & Brewer) publish books open access for a book processing charge. Ask for a quote early: for a full book, the fee is often the largest single cost.
- Open Book Publishers charges no mandatory fee, and African Minds covers costs itself for some titles.
- Ask your university library about open-access book funds, and include open-access costs in any RJ application. RJ allows such costs in its grants; for example, Flexit projects granted from 2020 onwards can use funds for open access and production for up to three years after the project ends. [RJ](https://www.rj.se/en/research/final-reports/flexit/)

## 6. Practical points

- **Host institution.** Several Swedish schemes require a Swedish university host, and Iso Lomso requires a position at an African institution. Settle your affiliations for 2027 first; the Gothenburg postdoc on your CV runs to 2026.
- **Hold workshops in Africa.** Visa costs and refusals often keep African scholars out of meetings in Europe and North America. A workshop on the continent, with an online option, avoids this and fits the book's purpose.
- **A senior co-editor helps.** Publishers and funders weigh the editors' track record. A senior Africa-based co-editor strengthens both the proposal and the grant applications.
- **Recruitment channels.** The AEHN newsletter, CODESRIA, the Economic History Society of Southern Africa, H-Africa and national historical associations. The AEHN holds an annual meeting for work in progress on African economic history; check where the 2027 meeting will be held and whether a panel or pre-conference workshop could host the volume.
